# E3 (redesigned) - Similarity discrimination

**Question:** Can the system tell a known attack, a modified variant and a previously unseen attack apart?

Sample: 90 synthetic incidents from 18 templates (5 each: 1 exact, 2 with one edit, 2 with two edits), one dataset, one seed; 399 next-step instances on ground-truth trajectories. Incidents of one template are not independent, so no confidence intervals are given; counts are shown instead. The first, 24-incident experiments are kept in experiments/results/.

**Metrics:** top1: the true next technique is the first prediction (ties broken by technique id). top1_tie: the same, but a target tied for first with m - 1 others counts 1/m, which removes alphabetical luck. top3: the target is among the first three. mrr: mean of 1/rank over the model's full ranking, 0 if the target is not ranked (the API exposes only the top 5). precision / recall / f1: macro-averaged over the techniques that occur as targets, using the top prediction only. An instance is one prefix of one incident's trajectory; an incident with L steps gives L - 1 instances.

## What the numbers show

- ground-truth trajectories: 60 of 78 classified correctly with the order-aware score (majority-class baseline 30; 65 with thresholds fitted on all incidents); 38 with the order-free score. 11 of 30 novel incidents were taken for a known or variant attack; near-novel recognised as novel 4 / 15, far-novel 15 / 15. AUC known vs variant 0.948; AUC (known + variant) vs novel 0.872.
- pipeline trajectories: 30 of 78 classified correctly with the order-aware score (majority-class baseline 30; 38 with thresholds fitted on all incidents); 40 with the order-free score. 12 of 30 novel incidents were taken for a known or variant attack; near-novel recognised as novel 3 / 15, far-novel 15 / 15. AUC known vs variant 0.594; AUC (known + variant) vs novel 0.638. Of the pipeline's incidents with no attack in them, 6 / 87 would be called a known or variant attack.
- Sensitivity to which six templates are held out (ground truth, 100 random sets): order-aware 51 to 76 of 78 correct (median 66); order-free 31 to 66 (median 50). The order-aware score was ahead in 100 of 100 sets. The set used here gives 60 and 38.

## Caveats

- What E3 supports: unrelated attacks are recognised as novel; the order-aware score is better than the order-free one across held-out choices. What it does not support: telling a near relative of a known attack from a variant of it, or anything on the label-free pipeline.
- Known versus variant is close to arithmetic. One edit to a 5-, 6- or 7-step template gives a similarity of 0.800 to 0.875 to its own reference by the definition of the score; two edits give less. The classifier is largely recovering the number of edits. Only the novel boundary is informative.
- Classes: known = an instance with ONE edit of a template that is in the history; variant = TWO edits; novel = any instance of the six templates left out of the history. The 12 exact (verbatim) instances of in-history templates are not classified; their scores are listed separately. This removes the tautology of the first E3, where 'known' meant a verbatim copy.
- Three of the six novel templates were chosen to be near relatives of in-history ones (G02 extends G01, G08 shares the phishing entry of G05 and the ending of G01, G13 reorders G04) and three to be unrelated. A system that calls a near relative 'variant' is arguably right; the near/far split shows where the novel errors are.
- The known / variant boundary is one edit vs two on sequences of 5 to 7 steps, a narrow margin by design.
- Thresholds are chosen by leave-one-out; the in-sample count shows how much they move.
- Which six templates are held out was fixed before any result was computed and not varied.
