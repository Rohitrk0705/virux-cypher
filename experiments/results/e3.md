# E3 - Similarity discrimination

**Question:** Can the system tell a known attack, a modified variant and a previously unseen attack apart?

Sample: 24 synthetic incidents from 4 attack templates (6 each), one dataset, one seed. No confidence intervals are given because the incidents are not independent draws; treat differences of a few points as noise.

## What the numbers show

- ground-truth trajectories: 22 of 24 incidents classified correctly with leave-one-out thresholds (majority-class baseline 9 of 24; 23 with thresholds fitted on all incidents). 0 of the 6 novel incidents were taken for a known or variant attack. Best-similarity ranges: known 1.000-1.000, variant 0.550-0.833, novel 0.343-0.550. Misclassified: GT-004 (B skip+insert, 0.633, variant called novel); GT-015 (A skip+insert, 0.550, variant called novel).
- pipeline trajectories: 13 of 24 incidents classified correctly with leave-one-out thresholds (majority-class baseline 9 of 24; 16 with thresholds fitted on all incidents). 2 of the 6 novel incidents were taken for a known or variant attack. Best-similarity ranges: known 0.000-1.000, variant 0.250-0.800, novel 0.250-0.571. Misclassified: GT-003 (C base, 0.250, known called novel); GT-007 (A skip, 0.250, variant called novel); GT-009 (B skip, 0.429, variant called novel); GT-011 (A insert, 0.800, variant called known); GT-013 (A base, 0.500, known called variant); GT-014 (B base, 0.000, known called novel); GT-015 (A skip+insert, 0.550, variant called novel); GT-016 (D base, 0.438, novel called variant); GT-018 (B insert, 0.714, variant called known); GT-019 (D base, 0.571, novel called variant); GT-024 (C skip+insert, 0.333, variant called novel). 1 incident(s) had no pipeline trajectory; they score 0 and are called novel. 2 correct 'known' verdict(s) (GT-017, GT-023) rest on a best match that is not the pattern's own reference, i.e. right for the wrong reason. Of the inferred incidents with no attack in them, 3 / 22 would be reported as a known or variant attack (2 / 22 with the order-free score; thresholds fitted on all 24).
- ground-truth trajectories, order-free (static) similarity instead: 24 of 24 correct against 22 with the order-aware score; the order-aware score is WORSE here.
- pipeline trajectories, order-free (static) similarity instead: 12 of 24 correct against 13 with the order-aware score; a one-incident difference, which is noise at this sample size.

## Caveats

- What E3 supports: on ground-truth trajectories one similarity score separates verbatim-known, variant and pattern-D incidents. What it does not support: usable discrimination on the label-free pipeline.
- 'Known' is 1.000 by construction: base incidents are verbatim copies of the reference sequences in the database, so the known row and 'AUC known vs rest' on ground truth are tautological.
- The variant/novel boundary is thin and depends on the hand-written distractors: on ground truth the lowest variant scores 0.550 and the highest novel incident 0.550. Pattern D's best matches are distractors written by the same author as the generator; without them D would score lower and separate more easily.
- Class sizes are 9 known, 9 variant, 6 novel. One incident changes accuracy by about 4 points.
- Leave-one-out thresholds avoid tuning on the tested incident but are still fitted to 23 incidents from the same 4 templates; the gap to the in-sample count shows how unstable they are.
- On pipeline trajectories, mapper and grouping errors lower the similarity of known attacks, and short fragments can equal very short MITRE group records.
- The pipeline also produces incidents that contain no attack at all. They are not part of the three-way accuracy; the summary table reports how many the in-sample thresholds would call a match.
- Similarity is computed on complete trajectories. For an attack still in progress the same measure is much weaker (see evaluation/results/m4_similarity.txt).
